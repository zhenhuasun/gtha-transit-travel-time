      Frequently asked questions

      How long does it take to cross Toronto by transit?

      From downtown (Yonge-Dundas Square), 10% of subway and streetcar stations are within 15 minutes and 36% within 30 minutes, walking and waiting included. The farthest, Long Branch Loop - Long Branch GO Station, is about 122 minutes away.

      How frequent are the subway and streetcar lines in Toronto?

      On a weekday during the day, the average interval between two services is Line 1 Yonge-University: 3.1 min, Line 2 Bloor-Danforth: 2.8 min, Line 4 Sheppard: 5.5 min, Line 5 Eglinton: 4.7 min, Line 6 Finch West: 7 min, streetcar 301: 10 min, streetcar 304: 10 min, streetcar 305: 10 min, streetcar 306: 10 min, streetcar 310: 10 min, streetcar 312: 10 min, streetcar 501: 10 min, streetcar 503: 7.3 min, streetcar 504: 9 min, streetcar 505: 6.1 min, streetcar 506: 10 min, streetcar 507: 7.4 min, streetcar 508: 30 min, streetcar 509: 9.1 min, streetcar 510: 6 min, streetcar 511: 6.2 min, streetcar 512: 6.2 min. The most frequent line is 2, with a service about every 2.8 minutes.

      Where do the timetables used come from?

      From the scheduled timetables published by the TTC network (GTFS format, Open Government Licence – Toronto), downloaded on 7 October 2026 and valid until 31 October 2026. Travel times are for Thursday 8 October 2026, between 7 a.m. and 8 p.m.

      Are buses included in Toronto?

      Yes, optionally: check "Bus" under the map. By default, only subway and streetcar are shown. Waiting time at infrequent bus stops is capped at 15 minutes.

      How are travel times calculated?

      For each trip: walk to the stop at 4.5 km/h, waiting time equal to half the interval between two services, scheduled time between stops, transfers with 1.5 minutes of walking, plus 1 minute to reach the subway platform. No real-time data or disruptions: this is the city "on paper".

      Who made this map of Toronto?

      The Toronto by Transit was created by Joshua Sun, using TTC schedules. The code is open on GitHub. The idea comes from NYC Transit Time Cartogram by Anthony Castrio, later adapted to Paris by Jules Grandin (C'est encore loin ?), then extended by Camille Roux — the concept and engineering are his work.
