      Frequently asked questions

      How long does it take to cross Greater Toronto and Hamilton Area by train?

      From downtown (Union Station), 8% of train, subway, LRT and streetcar stations are within 15 minutes and 34% within 30 minutes, walking and waiting included. The farthest, Niagara Falls GO, is about 171 minutes away.

      How frequent are the train, subway, LRT and streetcar lines in Greater Toronto and Hamilton Area?

      On a weekday during the day, the average interval between two services is Line 1 Yonge-University: 3.1 min, Line 2 Bloor-Danforth: 2.8 min, Line 4 Sheppard: 5.5 min, Barrie Line: 30 min, Kitchener Line: 30 min, Lakeshore East Line: 24.2 min, Lakeshore West Line: 28.9 min, Milton Line: 30 min, Richmond Hill Line: 30 min, Stouffville Line: 30 min, UP Express Line: 15 min, Line 5 Eglinton: 4.7 min, Line 6 Finch West: 7 min, ION light rail: 10.3 min, Streetcar 301: 10 min, Streetcar 304: 10 min, Streetcar 305: 10 min, Streetcar 306: 10 min, Streetcar 310: 10 min, Streetcar 312: 10 min, Streetcar 501: 10 min, Streetcar 503: 7.3 min, Streetcar 504: 9 min, Streetcar 505: 6.1 min, Streetcar 506: 10 min, Streetcar 507: 7.4 min, Streetcar 508: 30 min, Streetcar 509: 9.1 min, Streetcar 510: 6 min, Streetcar 511: 6.2 min, Streetcar 512: 6.2 min. The most frequent line is 2, with a service about every 2.8 minutes.

      Where do the timetables used come from?

      From the scheduled timetables published by the GO Transit network (GTFS format, Metrolinx Open Data Licence), downloaded on 15 October 2026. Travel times are for Thursday 15 October 2026, between 7 a.m. and 8 p.m.

      Are buses included in Greater Toronto and Hamilton Area?

      Yes, optionally. Waiting time at infrequent bus stops is capped at 15 minutes.

      How are travel times calculated?

      For each trip: walk to the stop at 4.5 km/h, waiting time equal to half the interval between two services, scheduled time between stops, transfers with 1.5 minutes of walking, plus 1 minute to reach the subway platform. No real-time data or disruptions.

      Who made this map of Toronto?

      The GTHA by Transit was created by Joshua Sun, using TTC schedules. The code is open on GitHub. The idea comes from NYC Transit Time Cartogram by Anthony Castrio, later adapted to Paris by Jules Grandin (C'est encore loin ?), then extended by Camille Roux — the concept and engineering are his work.
