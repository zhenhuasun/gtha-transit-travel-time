      Frequently asked questions

      How long does it take to cross Greater Toronto and Hamilton Area by train?

      From downtown (Union Station), 8% of train stations are within 15 minutes and 35% within 30 minutes, walking and waiting included. The farthest, Niagara Falls GO, is about 171 minutes away.

      How frequent are the train lines in Greater Toronto and Hamilton Area?

      On a weekday during the day, the average interval between two services is subway 1: 3.1 min, subway 2: 2.8 min, subway 4: 5.5 min, subway BR: 30 min, subway KI: 30 min, subway LE: 24.2 min, subway LW: 28.9 min, subway MI: 30 min, subway RH: 30 min, subway ST: 30 min, subway UP: 15 min, tram 5: 4.7 min, tram 6: 7 min, tram 301: 10 min, tram 304: 10 min, tram 305: 10 min, tram 306: 10 min, tram 310: 10 min, tram 312: 10 min, tram 501: 10 min, tram 503: 7.3 min, tram 504: 9 min, tram 505: 6.1 min, tram 506: 10 min, tram 507: 7.4 min, tram 508: 30 min, tram 509: 9.1 min, tram 510: 6 min, tram 511: 6.2 min, tram 512: 6.2 min. The most frequent line is 2, with a service about every 2.8 minutes.

      Where do the timetables used come from?

      From the scheduled timetables published by the GO Transit network (GTFS format, Metrolinx Open Data Licence), downloaded on 13 October 2026 and valid until 27 December 2026. Travel times are for Tuesday 13 October 2026, between 7 a.m. and 8 p.m.

      Are buses included in Greater Toronto and Hamilton Area?

      Yes, optionally: check "Bus" under the map. By default, only train are shown. Waiting time at infrequent bus stops is capped at 15 minutes.

      How are travel times calculated?

      For each trip: walk to the stop at 4.5 km/h, waiting time equal to half the interval between two services, scheduled time between stops, transfers with 1.5 minutes of walking, plus 1 minute to reach the subway platform. No real-time data or disruptions: this is the city "on paper".

      Who made this map of Greater Toronto and Hamilton Area?

      The idea comes from NYC Transit Time Cartogram by Anthony Castrio, later adapted to Paris by Jules Grandin (C'est encore loin ?), then extended by Camille Roux — the concept and engineering are his work. This Toronto edition is a fork by zhenhuasun, using TTC schedules. The code is open on GitHub.
